Horse or beef?

In 2013, nobody could say where the label changed.

The problem: horse meat sold as beef

  • In 2013, around 750 tonnes of horse meat were sold as beef in Europe*
  • It ended up in about 4.5 million products (Findus lasagne…), from 28 companies in 13 countries*
  • EU tests: 4.66 % of beef products had horse DNA (193 out of 4,144)**

And today?

Food fraud is still here: in a 2022 EU operation, 46 % of imported honey samples were adulterated, some with fake traceability documents***

Today

  • Certificates on paper or PDF, sent by email
  • Each company keeps its own system (ERP)
  • To trace a batch: phone calls and emails, one company at a time. It takes days (Walmart needed almost 7 days for mangoes*)
  • The law only asks each firm to know its supplier and its customer (“one step back, one step forward”)**, so nobody sees the whole chain
Our goal: trace any beef batch back to its abattoir in less than 1 hour, with every handover signed.

Our solution: MeatTrace

To make the case concrete, we imagined a client: FreshMeals, a French ready-meal company (fictional) with €120M sales, 6 beef suppliers in 3 countries and about 1,500 batches a year (our assumptions).

How it works for each batch

1. The sender records the batch and signs it
➜
2. The receiver checks it and confirms
➜
3. A lab tests some samples (DNA)
➜
4. Everyone allowed can trace the batch in minutes

What it brings

  • Faster: minutes instead of days
  • Safer: a relabelled batch is stopped at the next step
  • Fewer disputes: one shared history
  • Compliance with EU traceability rules

Why would partners join?

  • Retailers: proof of origin for customers
  • Honest suppliers: it's free, and it proves they were honest
  • FreshMeals: smaller recalls, faster audits
  • The lab: more tests to do

Users: abattoirs, traders, FreshMeals quality team, retailers, the DNA lab, the food authority (read only).

Business model and KPIs

Who pays?

  • We chose a consortium model: 4 founding members (FreshMeals, 2 retailers, the DNA lab) share all the costs, about €25k each per year (our estimate)
  • Suppliers join for free. We think getting them on board matters more than fees
  • Later, new buyers pay a membership fee

Rollout

Year 1: pilot with FreshMeals, 3 suppliers and the lab
Year 2: all 6 suppliers + 2 retailers
Year 3: other buyers of the same suppliers

Resources: IT partner, hosting of 4 nodes, security, training, support, a governance board.

When a database is enough

When one trusted company runs the whole process. Then it is cheaper and simpler, and we would choose it.

Why not here

6+ companies in 3+ countries, some of them competitors, blamed each other in 2013. Nobody would accept that a competitor controls the data.

Transactions, blocks, hash and signature

The 3 transactions we record

  • Slaughter: batch_id, species, weight_kg, site, date, signed by the abattoir
  • Transfer: batch_id, from, to, species_declared, weight_kg, signed by the sender, confirmed by the receiver
  • DNA test: batch_id, lab, result, hash of the lab report, signed by the lab
  • Hash (SHA-256): a 64-character fingerprint of these fields. One letter changes, the fingerprint changes
  • Signature: the sender signs with its private key, the others check with its public key. It proves who sent it and that it was not changed
  • Off-chain: prices, contracts and full lab reports stay with each company, only their hash is shared

Food authority❌❌Everything (audits)Observer

Consensus: 3 out of 4

The 4 validators (FreshMeals, 2 retailers, the lab) take turns proposing blocks. A block is accepted when 3 of 4 sign it (like PBFT), so one dishonest member can't fake or block it. We know all the members, so no mining (PoW) or staking (PoS).

Our rules

  • Identity: company certificate + personal login with 2FA
  • Nobody approves their own record
  • Confidential: prices and contracts stay off-chain
  • Revocation: people who leave are removed the same day
  • Board: the 4 founders, 1 vote each; new members or new rules need 3 votes; DNA results settle disputes

Main risks

  • False data entered: random DNA tests + the receiver must confirm
  • Data leaks: rights per role, prices stay off-chain
  • Members disagree: written rules, 3-of-4 vote
  • Costs too high: pilot first, database as plan B
  • Partners don't join: free for suppliers, simple website. TradeLens (300+ members) announced its closure in 2022 because the industry didn't join*

Our recommendation

Build MeatTrace as a permissioned consortium (FreshMeals, 2 retailers, a DNA lab): €305k over 3 years, €76k for FreshMeals.

First pilot goal: 100 % of pilot batches traced to the abattoir in under 1 hour.

Limits: it can't see inside the meat (we still need DNA tests), and without 3 co-funders we would go for the database. Still to build: real identities, nodes, website, security audit.
